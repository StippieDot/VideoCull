Duplicate Review finds exact or visually similar files across all loaded videos or the current filtered view. It groups the results, suggests one keeper per group, and gives you tools to mark likely copies. It never deletes files automatically. For an overview of what the duplicate video finder can and cannot detect, see [Find duplicate videos](https://videocull.app/find-duplicate-videos/) on the VideoCull website.

![VideoCull duplicate review comparing alternate Blender Open Movie encodes with similarity scores, metadata, playback controls, and suggested keepers](/screenshots/duplicate-review-dark.webp)

## Run detection

Duplicate detection needs at least two videos in its configured scope and waits for required metadata work to finish. Start it from the sidebar, or enable **Run after each scan** when duplicate review is the normal purpose of every session.

Choose **All loaded videos** to compare across every session root. Choose **Current filtered view** to limit work to the active grid filters, such as one folder, date range, rating, or status.

Exact-file checks are part of the run. The selected comparison method controls the sampled visual matching used for non-identical files:

| Method | Best use | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pHash (default) | Most libraries; strict image-signature comparison with few false positives | Can miss transformations that visual similarity still recognizes. |
| Visual similarity | Alternate encodes, resized, dimmed or recoloured copies, and near-identical footage. It compares where light and dark lie in each frame, not how bright the frames are, so dark scenes are not matched just for being dark. | Broader matching. Clips of the same scene filmed a moment apart can look alike; the **Outline check**, on at 75% by default, keeps most of them apart. |

The methods can find different results. Run them separately when coverage matters more than processing time: in duplicate groups, **Run Again with Visual Similarity** (or **with pHash**) in the sidebar, also in the **Duplicates** menu in the title bar, switches to the other method, saves it as your setting and runs detection again. **Undo** in the notice that follows switches back.

## Tune matching

Similarity is the minimum score required to create a match. Raise it when unrelated videos appear together; lower it when known alternate encodes are missed. Visual results below roughly 95 percent deserve especially careful review.

Each group says how it matched: **Identical files** for byte-for-byte copies, or **Same content at 3 of 3 sampled moments** for similarity matches, where the first number is the fewest samples any match in the group was compared on. Notes such as **Black bars ignored**, **Mirrored**, **Partly unreadable** or **Lengths differ by 2.0 s** say what was needed to find the match. Two labels ask you to look before deleting, and such groups are listed after the others:

- **Single-frame match — check before deleting**: a match rests on one sample, because the others were dark, blank or unreadable, or because first-sample scoring is on.
- **Little movement — check before deleting**: both videos show nearly the same picture throughout, such as a slideshow or a recorded screen, so they may share only a still image.

In the list view, each copy that is not the suggested keeper says why, such as **Not suggested: lower resolution**. Point at **Suggested keeper** to see the rule that decided; a decision by file size, codec or file name is marked as a tie-break, because the pictures did not differ. The gallery view shows the same label on the keeper's card and marks the best and worse values of each card.

The score shown for a group is its weakest match, which is the same value the Similarity setting tests. A group shown at 91 percent therefore appears in any run at 91 percent or lower, and lowering the setting only adds matches that score below the previous value. Hover a group's score to also see the average of its matches.

Sample count controls how many points in each video contribute evidence. More samples take longer but reduce the chance that one common frame, intro, or title card creates a false match.

**Ignore black bars** also matches a copy that only differs by black bars above, below or beside the picture. Each video is then compared both as it is and without its bars, and the better score counts.

**Partial clips** is a separate search for videos that share only part of their footage, such as an excerpt cut from a longer recording or a trimmed version. See [Partial clips](/features/partial-clips) for how it finds them and how to read its results.

**Double-check near misses** catches copies that start a moment earlier or later. Such a copy is sampled a fraction of a second off and scores below your similarity value, like an unrelated video would. Pairs that score up to 20 points below it (the range is adjustable; Visual similarity uses at most 10) are compared again with frames from one second before to one second after each sample. The pair matches only when one consistent time shift brings it to your similarity value; its group then says **Starts a moment earlier or later**. The extra frames are read once per video and kept. A run reads them for at most 200 videos, those with the most near misses first; if near misses are left, the sidebar says how many and **Check remaining near misses** checks them all in one run, which you can pause or cancel like any other.

Advanced controls handle harder libraries:

- Sampling windows can avoid repeated intros and outros, including a custom percentage range.
- **Length difference** limits how different runtimes may be before comparison.
- Requiring every sample scores each match by its weakest sample; turning it off scores and tests the average of all samples.
- Blank frames, a single flat colour such as a grey card, are skipped by default, because they would match any other blank frame.
- A video whose file is damaged at some sampled positions is compared on the samples that could be read. **Samples needed for a match** sets how many that must be; a match decided by one sample is marked for checking.
- Flipped comparison checks horizontally mirrored copies.
- Maximum sampling duration limits work on long files; `0` uses the full duration.
- Checkpoint intervals save progress during long runs, and retry can revisit failed fingerprints.

Change one matching control at a time, then run detection again. Existing groups are results from the previous run; changing a setting does not silently rebuild them.

When you reopen a session, the groups of its last run come back after the scan, together with any keepers you chose yourself. The sidebar shows when they were found and how many videos were added since. Videos deleted or changed in the meantime are taken out, and each group is rebuilt from the matches that are left, so two copies that were only linked through a deleted video no longer appear together. Changing a setting that decides which videos match, such as Similarity or the number of samples, discards the stored groups; run detection again. If part of the session could not be scanned, for example because a drive is not connected, nothing is restored until it can be.

## Read and filter results

Switch between compact rows and thumbnail gallery cards from the duplicate sidebar. Both show group boundaries, exact or potential-match badges, similarity, status, selected keeper, and useful quality metadata.

Duplicate-list filters are independent from the main grid filters. Search duplicate paths or filenames, set a minimum similarity to hide groups whose weakest match scores lower, and sort groups by similarity, group size, or total size. **Reset duplicate filters** returns to all groups sorted by highest similarity.

Play any result to open Review at that video with only its duplicate group as the navigation scope. Returning restores the duplicate-list scroll position.

## Understand keeper suggestions

The selected keeper is a recommendation, not an approval. The automatic ranking compares the configured priority order: resolution, video bitrate, duration, FPS, file size, and codec by default. The first unequal rule wins.

**Protect marked keep videos** removes already-kept files from automatic delete suggestions. **Protect skipped videos** does the same for Skipped files when enabled. Protection affects selection suggestions; it does not make the underlying match more accurate.

Right-click a video and choose **Set as selected keeper** when human context outweighs the automatic ranking. Clear the manual override from the group menu to use automatic ranking again.

## Select and mark copies

<Steps>
  <Step title="Inspect each group">
    Compare thumbnails, similarity, paths, duration, resolution, bitrate, FPS, and size. Play uncertain files before accepting a suggestion.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select suggested deletions">
    Choose **Select suggested deletions** to select non-keeper videos from the currently visible groups. Review the selected count and individual checks before continuing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Correct the selection">
    Toggle individual rows or cards, change the selected keeper, or clear the selection. Right-click a group to select suggestions for only that group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark selected as Delete">
    This changes statuses to Delete and leaves files on disk. Return to the grid, filter by Delete, and run the normal confirmed delete action only after the batch is correct.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Status protection and keeper suggestions do not lock a file. Targeted context-menu actions can mark a video Keep, Delete, or Pending when you intentionally need an override.

## Ignore false matches

Use **Not a match** after selecting exactly two videos from the same group. The pair is saved in ignored-match settings and excluded from later duplicate runs. In a two-video group, the current group disappears immediately; in a larger group, rerun detection to rebuild the remaining relationships.

Right-click actions provide broader corrections:

- **Exclude from group** ignores one video against every other video in that group.
- **Dismiss group** ignores every pair in the group and hides it from future runs.
- The toast **Undo** action restores the ignored pairs and current group while the toast remains available.
- **Clear ignored matches** in Settings allows all saved pairs to be considered again on the next run.

Use ignored matches for definite false positives. If many groups are wrong in the same way, tune the matcher and rerun instead of dismissing them one by one.

<Warning>
  Duplicate status marks feed the same global delete batch as grid and Review decisions. The final delete action includes all loaded videos marked Delete, even if current filters hide them.
</Warning>
